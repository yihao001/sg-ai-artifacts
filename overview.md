There are 3 key sources of information that gives you a strong foundation to most projects:

1. Open datasets on [`data.gov.sg`](https://data.gov.sg) is often a good starting point.
2. However, there are many non-open goverment datasets that need registration / API key (e.g. [LTA datamall](https://datamall.lta.gov.sg/content/datamall/en/request-for-api.html), [OneMap](https://www.onemap.gov.sg/apidocs/register), [URA](https://www.ura.gov.sg/property-data/data-release-calendar/), [MAS](https://eservices.mas.gov.sg/apimg-portal/api-catalog)). In this regard, you might find this [index](https://singaporeapi.com/apis?pricing=free&official=true) useful and it even comes with an [MCP server](https://singaporeapi.com/#agents) for your coding agents. Some individual stat boards / agencies have their own MCP too e.g. [SingStat](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/data-tools-services/singstat-mcp). Other MCPs of varying coverage: [1](https://github.com/prezgamer/Singapore-Data-MCPs), [2](https://mcpservers.org/servers/vdineshk/sg-data-mcp).
3. Check whether your application domain has any overlaps with what researchers in local universities are working on - they open-source their work (data, checkpoints) to varying extents and sometimes you have to contact them to get it. Here are some better examples: 
    - Urban Analytics Lab in NUS has a [guide to open urban data in SG](https://ual.sg/project/open-urban-data-singapore/), a Python library called [Urbanity](https://urbanity.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) for urban graph analytics and datasets such as [Global Streetscapes](https://ual.sg/project/global-streetscapes/) which includes SG as well.
    - [Building and Urban Data Science](https://budslab.org/) lab in SMU ran a Kaggle challenge which uses data such as physiological vitals, weather info and building morphology to predict human comfort metrics (i.e. noisy, hot).
